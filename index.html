FAQ

Why does macOS ask for audio permission?

To change an app's volume, MacMixer routes that app's sound through its own volume control, and macOS requires your permission for that. The sound is processed in real time on your Mac. Nothing is recorded, stored or sent anywhere.

Is MacMixer safe?

Yes. It's signed and notarized by Apple, uses Apple's own audio APIs and installs no drivers or system extensions. There's no account, and your audio never leaves your Mac.

How does the free trial work?

Download MacMixer and use every feature for 7 days, no payment details needed. When the trial ends, the app stops adjusting volumes and all sound goes back to normal until you activate a license.

How do I activate my license?

After you buy, your license key arrives by email. Open MacMixer › Settings › License, paste the key and click Activate. You can deactivate it on one Mac to move it to another.

Do I need the browser extension?

No. Without it, MacMixer controls each browser as a whole. With it, every tab gets its own slider. In Safari, the extension comes with the app; in Chrome and Arc, it takes about a minute to load in developer mode, with the steps in MacMixer › Settings.

How is it different from SoundSource?

SoundSource is a powerful pro tool with effects, EQ, volume boost and per-app output devices. MacMixer does one thing: simple volume control for each app and each browser tab, for $9.99. If you need EQ and audio routing, SoundSource is excellent; if you just want to turn things down, MacMixer is lighter and cheaper.

There are free alternatives. Why pay?

Does it work offline?

Yes. MacMixer checks your license once a day when you're online, and keeps working for up to 30 days without a connection.
